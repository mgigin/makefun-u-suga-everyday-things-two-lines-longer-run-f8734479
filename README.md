## The entry

> A tap is a little waterfall above your kitchen sink that starts pouring the moment you twist its handle.
> It maps the falling water and switching the flow on and off with a twist, but leaves out that a real waterfall never stops, is fed by a river far away, and would flood your whole kitchen.

Line 1 explains one everyday thing — a tap — to a 10-year-old in a single
vivid sentence built on one analogy: a little waterfall. Line 2 says in one
sentence why that analogy works: what it maps (falling water, switching the
flow on and off with a twist) and what it leaves out (a real waterfall never
stops, is fed by a river far away, and would flood the kitchen).
